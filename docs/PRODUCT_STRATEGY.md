# Reserve AI Product Strategy

Last updated: April 29, 2026

## 1. Executive Summary

Reserve AI should be positioned as a booking recovery and automation product for service businesses. The sharpest product promise is simple: when a team is too busy to answer the phone, Reserve AI answers, qualifies the customer, suggests available options, books or captures the lead, and reports what revenue was recovered.

The newest website already points in the right direction. It has a dark green AI visual system, a live orbital demo, booking-focused messaging, industry pages, and three subscription tiers. The remaining strategy work is to keep the product narrative focused. The website still offers Reserve POS, websites, and chatbots, but those should support the core booking assistant instead of making the company look like a broad agency.

Recommended positioning:

Reserve AI is a 24/7 AI booking assistant for restaurants, salons, barbershops, tattoo studios, nail studios, wellness centers, and spas. It answers missed calls, handles booking requests, captures leads, and connects to the business's existing calendar or booking tools.

## 2. Product Vision

Reserve AI becomes the default AI front desk for local service businesses.

The product should feel like an extra team member, not like complicated software. Owners should understand the value in one sentence: fewer missed calls, more confirmed bookings, calmer staff.

Long-term vision:

- Reserve AI answers voice calls, website chat, and social booking requests through one assistant.
- The assistant knows each business's services, opening hours, availability rules, tone, policies, and booking constraints.
- Owners can see missed-call recovery, confirmed bookings, transcripts, lead status, and revenue impact in a dashboard.
- Reserve AI expands from inbound call handling into follow-ups, reminders, reactivation, and customer retention.

## 4. Target Customer

Primary ideal customer profile:

- Local service business that depends on appointments, bookings, or reservations.
- The team is often busy in front of customers and misses phone calls.
- Average booking value is high enough that recovering a few missed calls pays for the subscription.
- The business already uses a calendar, POS, booking system, spreadsheet, or manual diary.
- The owner cares about customer experience but does not want to hire more front-desk staff.
- The business operates in Germany or serves German-speaking and English-speaking customers.

Priority industries:

- Restaurants and cafes
- Hair salons
- Barbershops
- Nail studios
- Tattoo studios
- Massage and wellness centers
- Spas and beauty businesses

Best early adopters:

- Owner-operated businesses with 2 to 20 employees.
- Businesses with at least 20 missed or unanswered calls per week.
- Businesses where each booking is worth EUR 40 to EUR 150+.
- Businesses that already pay for booking software or staff time.
- Businesses with simple repeatable booking workflows.

Poor-fit customers:

- Businesses with almost no phone demand.
- Businesses that require complex medical, legal, or financial advice on calls.
- Businesses that cannot define opening hours, services, prices, or booking rules.
- Businesses that expect the AI to replace all human customer service immediately.
- Businesses with extremely low booking value unless call volume is very high.

## 5. Buyer Personas

Owner/operator:

- Wants fewer missed bookings and less chaos.
- Makes the buying decision.
- Cares about cost, setup time, reliability, and customer perception.
- Needs proof that the product pays for itself.

Front-desk or floor team:

- Wants fewer interruptions during service.
- Cares about clean handoff, simple notifications, and not being replaced.
- Needs the assistant to be accurate and easy to correct.

Marketing or growth partner:

- Wants a clear lead capture and conversion system.
- Cares about website CTA, demo conversion, follow-up speed, and ROI reporting.
- Needs clean data on leads, calls, and booked appointments.

## 6. Core Problem

Service businesses lose bookings because customers call at the worst possible time.

Typical situation:

- A customer calls during lunch rush, a haircut, a tattoo session, or a treatment.
- Staff cannot answer without hurting the in-person customer experience.
- The caller hangs up and books somewhere else.
- The owner cannot see how many bookings were lost or what revenue was missed.
- Hiring a full-time receptionist is expensive and may not cover evenings or peak spikes.

Reserve AI should frame the problem as lost revenue and staff interruption, not as "AI transformation."

## 8. Product Positioning

Category:

AI booking assistant / AI receptionist for service businesses.

What it is:

- A voice-first assistant for booking calls and reservation requests.
- A system for capturing leads when staff cannot answer.
- A lightweight automation layer around the business's existing booking workflow.

What it is not:

- Not a generic chatbot.
- Not a replacement for all staff.
- Not a broad software agency offer.
- Not a fully custom enterprise call center on day one.

Positioning statement:

For local service businesses that lose bookings when the phone is missed, Reserve AI is a 24/7 AI booking assistant that answers calls, captures reservation requests, and connects to the business's existing workflow. Unlike generic chatbots or call centers, Reserve AI is configured around local booking rules, German/English service expectations, and fast small-business onboarding.

## 9. Product Architecture

Core product modules:

- AI voice assistant: answers inbound calls, understands intent, responds naturally, and follows business rules.
- Booking intake: collects customer name, preferred date/time, service, party size, staff preference, and notes.
- Availability logic: suggests available or alternative slots based on configured rules or integrated systems.
- Confirmation flow: confirms bookings by SMS, email, calendar event, or internal notification depending on setup.
- Handoff logic: escalates unusual requests to the owner or team by email/SMS/dashboard.
- Business knowledge base: services, prices, opening hours, cancellation policy, FAQs, location, parking, language tone.
- Reporting: call volume, answered calls, missed-call recovery, booked leads, unresolved requests, estimated value.
- Admin setup: business profile, services, availability, integrations, voice tone, languages, fallback rules.

Service add-ons:

- Website build or modernization.
- Reserve POS setup or booking-system integration.
- Website chatbot.
- Social media booking setup.
- CRM or POS integration.

Strategic rule:

The core product should always be sold first. Add-ons should be framed as implementation accelerators, not separate company identities.

## 10. Minimum Viable Product

MVP scope:

- AI answers inbound calls.
- AI handles common booking and reservation questions.
- AI collects required customer information.
- AI suggests availability from a configured schedule or business rules.
- AI sends the request or booking to the business.
- AI captures calls it cannot complete and notifies the team.
- Owner can review call outcomes and transcripts.
- Basic setup can be completed within 24 to 48 hours for simple businesses.

MVP success criteria:

- A business can go live without technical staff.
- The assistant can handle the top 10 booking questions confidently.
- The assistant can fail safely and hand off instead of hallucinating.
- The owner can see enough results to justify the monthly plan.
- The demo can convert interested prospects into sales calls.

Do not overbuild early:

- Do not build a complex enterprise dashboard before call reliability and booking outcomes are strong.
- Do not promise every POS integration before repeatable integration playbooks exist.
- Do not sell fully custom workflows at low prices.

## 11. Key User Journeys

Prospect journey:

1. Prospect lands on the website after seeing outreach, referral, ad, or social content.
2. Prospect understands the problem: missed calls during service create lost revenue.
3. Prospect sees a live AI booking demo.
4. Prospect submits contact details to test the demo or book a free consultation.
5. Reserve AI follows up with a missed-call audit and recommended package.
6. Prospect receives proposal with monthly plan, setup fee, workflow scope, and launch timeline.

Customer onboarding journey:

1. Customer completes business intake form.
2. Reserve AI collects services, prices, opening hours, booking rules, FAQs, calendar/POS details, and preferred tone.
3. Team configures assistant and fallback rules.
4. Test calls are completed with the owner.
5. Phone forwarding or number connection is activated.
6. First-week monitoring catches mistakes and updates the assistant.
7. Customer receives weekly performance summary.

Caller journey:

1. Customer calls the business.
2. Reserve AI answers in the correct tone and language.
3. AI identifies intent: booking, availability, cancellation, pricing, location, or general question.
4. AI completes the booking or captures all required details.
5. AI confirms the next step and sets customer expectations.
6. Business receives the booking, lead, or handoff notification.

## 12. Pricing Strategy

Current website pricing:

- Starter: EUR 249/month, up to 150 voice minutes/month.
- Growth: EUR 599/month, up to 500 voice minutes/month.
- Pro: EUR 1290/month, up to 1,500 voice minutes/month.
- Setup: starts at EUR 500 depending on workflow complexity.
- Terms: no long-term contracts, cancel anytime, 14-day money-back guarantee.

Pricing rationale:

The product should be priced against recovered bookings, not against cheap software tools. If the average booking value is EUR 50 to EUR 100, recovering only a few missed calls can cover the monthly plan.

Recommended packaging:

- Starter: simple call answering and booking intake for small teams.
- Growth: best default plan for serious local businesses with steady call volume.
- Pro: high-volume businesses, advanced workflows, integrations, and outbound recovery.

Recommended add-ons:

- Additional voice minutes.
- Additional languages.
- Advanced POS or CRM integration.
- Website chatbot.
- Website rebuild or conversion landing page.
- Social media booking automation.
- Monthly optimization and reporting package.

Sales guardrails:

- Never include unlimited custom workflow work in the monthly price.
- Quote integration complexity before signing.
- Keep setup fee high enough to cover discovery, configuration, testing, and launch.
- Use Growth as the recommended plan when the business has meaningful call volume.

## 13. Go-To-Market Strategy

Primary go-to-market motion:

Direct local-business sales supported by a strong demo, industry-specific landing pages, and partner/referral relationships.

Channels:

- Cold email and Instagram outreach to restaurants, salons, barbershops, tattoo studios, nail studios, wellness centers, and spas.
- Local SEO landing pages for "AI receptionist for salons", "AI booking assistant for restaurants", and similar keywords.
- Short demo videos showing real call scenarios.
- Partnerships with website designers, local marketing agencies, POS consultants, and booking software consultants.
- Referrals from early customers.

Lead magnet:

Free missed-call recovery audit.

Audit promise:

We estimate how many bookings your business could be losing from missed calls and show how Reserve AI would handle them.

Sales motion:

1. Lead submits demo or audit request.
2. Qualify by industry, call volume, booking value, and current booking system.
3. Run a 20-minute discovery call.
4. Show a personalized demo script using their business type.
5. Present ROI estimate and recommended plan.
6. Collect setup payment and schedule onboarding.

Internal roles:

## 19. Risks And Mitigations

Risk: Website looks like a broad agency.

Mitigation: Put the booking assistant first. Move other services into add-ons.

Risk: AI gives wrong answers.

Mitigation: Use a strict knowledge base, fallback rules, call testing, and human handoff.

Risk: Integrations become too custom.

Mitigation: Start with defined integration tiers and quote complex work separately.

Risk: Customers expect instant perfect automation.

Mitigation: Set expectations around setup, testing, first-week tuning, and human handoff.

Risk: Pricing feels high.

Mitigation: Sell against recovered revenue, not software cost. Use missed-call audit and ROI examples.

Risk: Demo creates interest but weak qualification.

Mitigation: Add business type, phone, booking system, and call volume fields to the demo gate.
